Robotic welding cell: upgrade and integration

ChrimaManufacturing Systems SpecialistSummer 2026
Problem
The robotic welding cell was upgraded to improve weld quality, as part of the shop's modernization project.
Constraints
  • Custom-built FANUC robot
  • Third-party Fronius welding package
  • Brought into a working production shop
Solution
  • Fronius welder fitted to the robot
  • Wire cutter and nozzle cleaner added
  • Helped with the upgrade and integration
Result
After the upgrade, the FANUC robot runs full weld cycles on the fixture table using the Fronius power source. The wire cutter and nozzle cleaner trim the wire and clear spatter between welds, so each arc starts from the same condition.

On-floor weld inspection with optical metrology

ChrimaManufacturing Systems SpecialistSummer 2026
Problem
Weld checks on awkward geometry had to queue at the metrology department, delaying rework decisions.
Constraints
  • Hard-to-measure geometry
  • Scans picked up the surface the part sat on
  • Run by weld managers, not metrology staff
  • Delivered within one work term
Solution
  • Integrated a Revopoint TrackIt tracker and scanner
  • Designed and built highly reflective support pins the scanner barely registers, separating the part from the table
  • Scanning fixtures, SOPs and staff training
Result
Weld managers now scan awkward geometry on the floor and decide on rework themselves, without queuing parts at metrology. Reflective support pins remove table noise from each scan, making scans faster and more accurate.

Founding an adult athletic league: AI client assistant development and integration

Milton Ivy LeagueCo-FounderFeb 2023 – present
Overview
An adult recreational hockey league in Milton, Ontario, founded in 2023 and grown to 220 players by more than doubling each year. To manage growth, we integrated Conrad, an AI assistant built into our systems. Players can ask it anything from who to contact to scheduling, and Conrad handles all check-in interactions and guides players on where to go on game day.
Constraints
  • Small staff
  • Fixed operating budget
  • Error-prone manual records
  • 220 players arriving across game nights
  • Unreliable Wi-Fi at the rink
Solution
  • Online player statistics system
  • Built Conrad, an AI client assistant that runs fully offline on a Raspberry Pi with a check-in desktop
  • Players sign in with personal codes and are shown their dressing room and rink
  • Automatic registration, with staff checking who has and hasn't registered
  • First-aid stock tracking: staff log an item as used and Conrad reports what is left
Result
The league grew to 220 players in two years, more than doubling each year, with fewer administrative errors once statistics moved online. At the playoff tournament, Conrad's self check-in saved about 20 hours of labour across three staff compared with the year before.
